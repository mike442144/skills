# Chemical Industry Cycle Analysis Framework

This reference provides a structured framework for analyzing the cyclical position of chemical industry companies, with specific patterns for MDI/polyurethane sub-sector. Use as an appendix or supplement to Chapter 3 (Industry & Competitive Landscape) when the target company is in a cyclical chemical sub-sector.

## Cycle Position Confirmation Signals

Four bottom-confirmation indicators to check (as of mid-2026):

| Signal | What to Check | Historical Context |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|
| PPI negative streak | Search "化工PPI 连续 同比负增长" | 2012-2016 was 54 months; 2022-2026 is the 2nd longest |
| CIP (在建工程) growth | Check SW基础化工在建工程同比增速 | Turns negative = capex cycle ended |
| Fixed asset investment | 化学原料制品制造业固投完成额同比 | Turns negative = expansion cycle ended |
| Price percentile | Track 100+ chemical product price percentiles | 70+ products below 30th percentile = bottom zone |

## MDI Sub-Sector Cycle Pattern (25-year history)

MDI cycle is driven by capacity iteration. The规律 is consistent:
1. Capacity growth stalls → prices rise
2. Price rises → attract new capacity investment
3. New capacity peaks → prices enter decline
4. New capacity investment decreases → cycle restarts

**Historical rounds (for context in reports):**
- 1994-2002: Supply loosening, prices drift lower
- 2002-2010: 2003-04 capacity stall → price surge → 2005+ capex + financial crisis → bottom
- 2010-2016: 2010 low additions → price rise → Ningbo 600kt expansion → 2015-16 oil crash → bottom
- 2016-present: 2016-17 slow additions + force majeure → price surge → 2018+ Sadara 400kt + trade war → sustained low

**Key price percentile reference (中泰证券, early 2026, vs 2016 baseline):**
- Pure MDI price: ~20th percentile, spread: ~21st percentile
- Polymeric MDI price: ~33rd percentile, spread: ~27th percentile
- TDI price: ~40th percentile, spread: ~54th percentile

## Supply-Side Structural Changes (Current Cycle)

**Domestic capex cycle end:**
- CIP growth turned negative 2025Q1, reached -15% by 2025Q3
- Fixed asset growth peaked 2024Q1, declining since
- Capex growth negative for 4+ consecutive quarters
- Chemical FIA turned negative June 2025 (first time in 5 years)

**Overseas high-cost capacity exit:**
- Europe: 7 cracker units closed/planned closure since 2024 (4M tons ethylene)
- ExxonMobil UK 830kt steam cracker permanently closed Feb 2026
- Huntsman Netherlands 280kt MDI unit force majeure Nov 2025
- BASF Europe ethane shortage → potential load reduction 2026Q1
- Japan/Korea naphtha crackers utilization declining
- EU CBAM carbon border tax increasing European chemical costs

**Key insight (国盛证券):** "我国许多化工品已经供应了全球超过一半的需求，因此供给周期成为了判断盈利周期的核心。历史上化工板块利润率和在建工程增速确认拐点的季度具有较强的反向同步性。"

## Demand-Side Transition

**Headwinds:** Real estate (MDI building insulation 50% globally, only 22% China), traditional textiles (<3% growth)

**Tailwinds:** Auto/appliances (low-double-digit growth), new energy materials (15-20% growth), exports (volume index 122+), MDI new applications (formaldehyde-free boards, PV frames, auto lightweighting)

**MDI consumption规律:** Global MDI consumption growth ≈ 2x GDP growth. China 2019-2025 MDI CAGR 10.9%.

## Oil Price Impact on Chemical Companies

| Benefiting | Logic |
|-----------|-------|
| Ethane-route olefins | Cost insulated from oil; product follows oil-head pricing |
| Coal-chemical route | High oil → coal-to-olefins cost advantage widens |
| Oligopoly pricing power | Cost-push can be passed through to customers |

| Pressured | Logic |
|-----------|-------|
| Naphtha-route petrochemicals | Input cost rises; margins compress if demand weak |
| Downstream processing | Unable to pass cost through to end consumers |

## Cycle Position Summary Framework

When writing the appendix, structure as:
1. Where are we now (signals dashboard)
2. How did we get here (cycle timeline)
3. Sub-sector specifics (MDI/other)
4. Supply-side changes (domestic + overseas)
5. Demand transition (headwinds vs tailwinds)
6. Key variable (oil price / geopolitics)
7. Consensus view vs dissent
8. Company-specific implications (resilience proof + upside elasticity)
