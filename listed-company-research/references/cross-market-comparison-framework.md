# Cross-Market Comparison Framework

## When to Trigger

Proactively initiate a cross-market comparison when the target company faces **structural headwinds** that have already played out in analogous markets:
- Population/birth rate decline (dairy, education, baby products, housing)
- Digital disruption (print media, physical retail, traditional banking)
- Industry maturity / market saturation
- Regulatory tightening cycles
- Technology paradigm shifts

The comparison is most valuable when the headwind is **structural and irreversible** (not cyclical), and when analogous markets are **10-30 years ahead** in experiencing the same trend.

## Analytical Framework

### Step 1: Define the Inflection Point

For each comparable company/market, identify the specific inflection point:
- **Demographic:** Year when birth rate/population began sustained decline (e.g., Japan 2005, Korea 2012, China 2016)
- **Industry:** Year when market size peaked or began sustained contraction
- **Regulatory:** Year when key regulation took effect

### Step 2: Profit Tracking Table (Core Deliverable)

For each comparable company, construct a long-term profit trajectory table spanning **at least 15-20 years** post-inflection. Key metrics:

| Year | Revenue | Operating Profit | Operating Margin | Net Profit | Key Milestones |
|------|---------|-----------------|-----------------|------------|----------------|

**Why operating profit over net profit:**
- Net profit is distorted by one-time items (asset impairments, investment gains/losses, restructuring charges)
- Operating profit reflects the underlying business economics
- Always show operating margin to track whether the company is extracting more value per unit of revenue

### Step 3: Summary Comparison Matrix

| Company | Country | Years Since Inflection | Operating Profit Change | Margin Change | Core Strategy |
|---------|---------|----------------------|------------------------|---------------|---------------|

### Step 4: Strategy Classification

Classify each company's adaptation path into archetypes:
1. **Functionalization** — Upgrade basic products to premium/functional (e.g., Meiji's LB81/LG21 yogurt)
2. **Full Lifecycle Extension** — Extend from core demographic to all ages (e.g., Meiji's elderly nutrition)
3. **Globalization** — Enter markets where the headwind hasn't hit yet (e.g., Yakult in 40 countries)
4. **Upstream/B2B Pivot** — Shift from selling brands to selling ingredients/technology (e.g., FrieslandCampina whey protein)
5. **Adjacent Industry Crossover** — Enter pharma/health science (e.g., Meiji's pharmaceutical segment, Danone's Nutricia)
6. **Single Product Globalization** — Take one killer product worldwide (e.g., Yakult)

### Step 5: Mapping to Target Company

For each strategy archetype, assess the target company's:
- Current progress (0-100% scale)
- Time window remaining (how fast is the headwind approaching?)
- Key capability gaps vs. successful precedents
- Most critical risk of failure

## Data Collection Strategy

**Primary sources for foreign company financial data (priority order):**
1. Company IR pages — annual results PDFs, financial highlights pages
2. WSJ / Yahoo Finance / Bloomberg — income statement history
3. Integrated reports — often contain 10-year financial summaries
4. Brokerage research reports — sometimes compile long-term data
5. Industry association publications

**Search patterns:**
- `[Company name] operating profit history [year range] annual results`
- `[Company name] financial results long term trend`
- Japanese companies: Search for `FY[year] financial results PDF` on company IR page (they typically archive 10+ years)
- Korean companies: Use `mk.co.kr` (Maeil Business), `chosun.com` for Chinese-language coverage
- European companies: Annual reports on company investor relations pages

**Common data gaps:**
- Japanese companies use March fiscal year (FY2025 = April 2025 to March 2026) — clarify in tables
- Korean companies often have limited English disclosure — use Korean financial media + Google Translate
- Private companies / subsidiaries may only have partial data — note gaps explicitly
- Historical data beyond 15 years is often unavailable online — use proxy (brokerage reports with compiled histories)

## Key Findings from Prior Research

### Japan Dairy (2005 inflection point, 21 years post):
- **Meiji:** Operating profit +210%, margin 3%→8%. Most successful. Key: food+pharma dual-wheel, 50-year functionalization investment
- **Yakult:** Operating profit +130%, margin stable ~9%. Single-product globalization in 40 countries
- **Morinaga Milk:** Operating profit +245%, margin 2%→6%. B2B pivot (BB536 strain, lactoferrin via MILEI GmbH)
- **Megmilk Snow Brand:** Operating profit +140%, margin 1.6%→3.1%. Low-value categories (butter/cheese) limited functionalization

### Europe (2010 inflection point, 15 years post):
- **Danone:** Operating profit +61%, margin stable ~13%. Medical nutrition (Nutricia) became profit engine at 21.7% margin
- **Nestlé:** Closed infant formula factories, pivoted to Nestlé Health Science. Profit volatile due to portfolio restructuring
- **FrieslandCampina:** Operating profit ~0 growth, margin 5%→3.8%. Considering selling Friso brand, pivoting to B2B protein ingredients

### Korea (2013 inflection point, 12 years post):
- Market contracted 38% in 6 years
- **Namyang Dairy:** 5 consecutive years of losses, acquired by PE fund, restructured
- Key survival strategy: export to Southeast Asia with "ingredient competitiveness" positioning

### China Feihe (2016 inflection point, 9 years post):
- Profit "inverted V": +517% (2016→2020 peak), then -74% (2020→2025)
- Decline speed far exceeds Japan (9yr -56% births vs Japan 21yr -35%)
- Strategy resembles Meiji's vision but faces FrieslandCampina-like execution constraints

## Type 2: Financial Services / Market Structure Comparisons

**When to use:** When the target operates in financial services, fintech, or platform businesses where the key question is **market development stage, regulatory trajectory, or business model viability** rather than demographic headwinds.

**Key differences from Type 1:**
- Focus on **market-level metrics** (penetration rates, market size, interest rate regimes) rather than individual company profit trajectories
- Compare **regulatory environments** across jurisdictions (rate caps, commission rules, consumer protection)
- Benchmark **business models** (OEM captive vs bank direct vs fintech platform vs subprime specialist)
- Identify **best practices** from mature markets that could be replicated

**Structure for financial services comparisons:**

1. **Market Size & Penetration Table:**
   | Market | Market Size | Penetration Rate | Growth Stage | Key Characteristics |

2. **Market Structure & Participants:**
   - Who are the dominant players in each market? (banks, OEM captives, fintechs, credit unions)
   - What is the competitive landscape? (concentrated vs fragmented)
   - What is the market share of each player type?

3. **Interest Rate & Pricing Comparison:**
   | Country | Prime Rate | Subprime Rate | Regulatory Caps | Notes |

4. **Business Model Comparison:**
   | Model | Representative Companies | Core Advantage | Key Risk | Applicable Markets |

5. **Regulatory Environment Comparison:**
   | Country | Regulator | Key Rules | Impact on Platforms |

6. **Strategic Implications for Target:**
   - What can be learned from mature markets?
   - Which business models work best in which regulatory environments?
   - What is the target's competitive positioning vs international peers?

**Example from Yixin Group (02858.HK) session:**
- Compared auto finance markets: US (80%+ penetration), UK (85-90%, PCP dominant), Germany (OEM captive dominant), Japan (residual value loans), Australia (novated leases), China (40-45%, fastest growing)
- Key insight: UK FCA banned discretionary commission arrangements (DCA) in 2021, facing £9bn redress scheme — directly relevant to Yixin's commission-based model
- Japan's residual value loans (残価設定ローン) similar to UK PCP — potential product innovation for Yixin
- US Carvana/CarMax showed both opportunities (vertical integration) and risks (2022-23 near-bankruptcy from funding structure)

**Search patterns for financial services:**
- `[Country] auto finance market size penetration rate [year]`
- `[Country] consumer lending regulation interest rate cap`
- `[Company] business model annual report investor presentation`
- `FCA motor finance market analysis` (UK)
- `CFPB auto lending report` (US)
- `BaFin consumer credit regulation` (Germany)

**Data sources:**
- Experian State of Automotive Finance Market (US)
- FCA Market Analysis reports (UK)
- Central bank statistics (various countries)
- Company annual reports and investor presentations
- Industry association reports (ACEA for Europe, JADA for Japan)

## Writing Principles

1. **Always use operating profit, not net profit, for long-term comparisons** — net profit is too noisy for cross-company comparison
2. **Show the full trajectory, not just endpoints** — the path matters (Meiji's steady climb vs Feihe's inverted V)
3. **Quantify the speed of the headwind** — "birth rate fell X% in Y years" gives context for why some companies had more time to adapt
4. **Be explicit about data gaps** — if a company's historical data is unavailable, say so rather than omitting
5. **Map strategies to the target company's specific situation** — don't just list what others did; assess what's replicable given the target's constraints (time, capital, capabilities)
6. **Distinguish vision from execution** — a company may articulate a Meiji-like vision but face FrieslandCampina-like realities. Call this out explicitly.
7. **For financial services: show market development stage** — penetration rates and regulatory maturity matter more than individual company profits
8. **For financial services: compare regulatory trajectories** — identify emerging regulations in target market by looking at what mature markets have already implemented
